Reading list 01

Asset & Liability Management

Savings and retirement investment focus. Chapters pulled from the QuantEcon lecture series and ranked by relevance to modelling pension liabilities, decumulation, and long-horizon portfolio design.

First course — foundational intuition Intermediate — applied models Advanced — theory & robustness
01

Core Must-Read

Directly models ALM problems

ChapterSeriesWhy it matters for ALM
Ch 12 — Consumption SmoothingFIRSTCore ALM intuition: matching spending liabilities to asset income over time.
Ch 75 — The Permanent Income ModelINTERFormal model of optimal saving/consumption under income uncertainty — the backbone of retirement planning theory.
Ch 76 — Permanent Income II: LQ TechniquesINTERSolves the permanent income model computationally; shows how to implement lifecycle savings strategies.
Ch 85 — Consumption Smoothing with Incomplete and Complete MarketsINTERModels how access to financial instruments (bonds, annuities) changes optimal retirement portfolios.
Ch 67–68 — Income Fluctuation Problem I & IIINTERHousehold buffer-stock saving under income risk — directly relevant to defined-contribution retirement accounts.
Ch 61–65 — Optimal Savings I, II, III, IV, V (Cake Eating)INTERStep-by-step build-up of the finite-horizon drawdown problem; models decumulation (spending down a retirement pot).
Ch 94 — The Aiyagari ModelINTERHeterogeneous-agent model of wealth accumulation under uninsurable income risk — standard framework for pension fund design analysis.
02

Asset Pricing & Portfolio Theory

High value for ALM

ChapterSeriesWhy it matters for ALM
Ch 42 — Elementary Asset Pricing TheoryADVState-price deflators, no-arbitrage pricing — essential for valuing pension liabilities and annuities.
Ch 45 — Two Modifications of Mean-Variance Portfolio TheoryADVDirectly addresses portfolio construction; mean-variance is the standard ALM optimisation framework.
Ch 99 — Asset Pricing: Finite State ModelsINTERPricing bonds and equity in discrete settings; needed for liability-matching bond portfolios.
Ch 100 — Stochastic Consumption, Risk Aversion & Asset ReturnsINTERLinks investor risk aversion to required returns — core to setting long-run return assumptions in retirement plans.
Ch 44 — The Hansen-Jagannathan BoundADVBenchmark for evaluating whether a portfolio's risk/return is consistent with any rational preference model.
Ch 102 — Competitive Equilibria with Arrow SecuritiesINTERComplete markets benchmark for hedging longevity and income risks in pension design.
03

Risk, Uncertainty & Robustness

Context for ALM

ChapterSeriesWhy it matters for ALM
Ch 28 — Risk and Model UncertaintyADVDistinguishes risk (known distribution) from ambiguity — critical for long-horizon retirement planning where distributions are unknown.
Ch 30 — RobustnessADVHow to make savings/investment decisions that are robust to model misspecification — directly applicable to stress-testing ALM models.
Ch 82 — Robust Consumption Smoothing and Precautionary SavingsINTERPrecautionary saving motive under ambiguity — explains why retirement buffers exist beyond pure optimization.
Ch 34 — AR(1) ProcessesFIRSTFoundation for modelling interest rates, asset returns, and wage processes used in all lifecycle models.
Ch 38 — Finite Markov ChainsINTERModels regime-switching in asset returns and employment states — standard in stochastic ALM scenario generation.
Ch 98 — Two Computations to Fund Social SecurityINTERDirectly models public pension funding — most applicable chapter to institutional retirement fund management.
